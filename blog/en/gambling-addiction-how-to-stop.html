Gambling addiction: how to stop betting and get your life back

Sports betting apps put a casino in your pocket, open 24/7, with a "boost" notification every time you have a bad day. If you're here, you've probably already tried to cut back on your own. This guide skips the lecture and gives you a practical plan: blocking tools, money controls, debt, telling people, and what kind of help actually works.

If you're thinking about ending your life because of gambling losses, call or text 988 (US), call 116 123 (Samaritans, UK and Ireland), or your local emergency number.

Gambling has one of the highest suicide risks of any addiction. Debt feels permanent. It isn't.

Is it a problem, or just a bad run?

Most people with a gambling problem spend a long time believing they have a money problem. Look at behavior, not your balance. Common signs:

  • You chase losses: betting more to win back what you lost.
  • You bet more than you planned, or keep going after you said you'd stop.
  • You hide how much you bet from your partner, family, or yourself.
  • You've borrowed money, used credit, or skipped bills because of gambling.
  • You feel restless or irritable when you try to cut down.
  • You watch games mainly because you have money on them.

Clinicians call this gambling disorder, and it's recognized as a real addiction, alongside substance use disorders. That's useful to know: it means "just be more disciplined" was never going to be enough.

Why "one more win" is the trap

Every sportsbook and casino has a built-in house edge, so over time the result is fixed. But the bigger trap is psychological. Near-misses and occasional wins hit your brain's reward system harder than steady pay ever will. That's why people who "almost got it all back" usually end up deeper in the hole. The only place to stop is where you are right now, even down.

Today: make betting hard to do

You don't need to decide how to live the rest of your life without gambling. You need to make tonight's bet difficult.

  1. Delete every betting and casino app, and log out of the websites.
  2. Self-exclude. In the UK, GAMSTOP blocks you from all licensed online operators at once. In the US, self-exclusion is handled state by state, usually through your state's gaming regulator, plus each sportsbook's own "close account" or "self-exclusion" option. Pick the longest period offered.
  3. Install a blocker such as Gamban or BetBlocker on every device, and give the password to someone else.
  4. Turn on your bank's gambling block. Many banks and card apps have a setting that declines gambling transactions. If yours doesn't, call and ask.
  5. Unsubscribe from promo emails and texts, and unfollow betting and tipster accounts. Those "free bet" offers are aimed at exactly the moment you're weakest.

This week: put a speed bump between you and your money

Most people resist this step because it feels like losing independence. Think of it as temporary scaffolding. Ask someone you trust to help for the next 3–6 months:

  • Have your paycheck go to an account they can see or co-manage.
  • Keep only a small weekly spending amount on your card.
  • Freeze your credit with the credit bureaus (in the US this is free) so you can't open new credit on impulse.
  • Agree that any purchase above a set amount gets talked about first.

Dealing with the debt

Write down every debt: who, how much, interest rate, minimum payment, and whether anything is past due. Seeing the real number is scary, but it is finite, which is more than the version in your head.

  • Don't borrow to pay off gambling debt. It's another bet.
  • Prioritize essentials first: rent or mortgage, utilities, and anything that could lead to losing your home.
  • Call creditors before you miss payments and ask about hardship plans.
  • Talk to a nonprofit credit counselor (in the US, look for one affiliated with the NFCC) or, in the UK, a free debt charity like StepChange. Avoid companies that charge big upfront fees to "settle" your debt.

Telling the people close to you

Secrecy is what keeps a gambling problem alive. When you tell someone, say the hard part first and say all of it: how much you've lost, what you owe, and what you've hidden. Partial disclosure, with more coming out later, does more damage than one honest conversation. Then tell them what you've already done (deleted apps, self-excluded) and what you need from them. Expect anger. Give them time, and point them to Gam-Anon, which supports families.

Help that actually works

  • Helplines. In the US, the National Council on Problem Gambling runs a free, confidential helpline at 1-800-GAMBLER (call or text). In the UK, the National Gambling Helpline is run by GamCare.
  • Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT). The best-studied treatment for gambling disorder. It targets the thinking errors gamblers rely on, like "I'm due a win." Some insurance plans cover it; ask for a therapist who treats addiction.
  • Gamblers Anonymous. Free peer meetings, in person and online. You don't have to buy into everything to benefit from being in a room with people who understand.
  • Treat what's underneath. Depression, anxiety, ADHD, and drinking often travel with gambling. Treating those lowers the urge to bet.

Handling urges

Urges are predictable: payday, big games, boredom, after an argument, a few drinks in. List your top triggers and decide in advance what you'll do instead. When an urge hits, set a 20-minute timer and don't decide anything until it goes off. Call or text someone, go for a walk, or write down what set it off. Urges peak and fade if you don't feed them.

If you slip

A lapse doesn't erase your progress. What makes it dangerous is trying to win it back or hide it. Stop the same day, tell your support person, figure out how the bet got through (a new app, a second card, a friend's account), and close that gap.

The short version

Today: delete apps, self-exclude, install a blocker, turn on your bank's gambling block. This week: put money controls in place and list your debts. This month: tell someone and get real support, whether that's a helpline, CBT, or GA. You don't need to win it back. You just need to stop losing more.
